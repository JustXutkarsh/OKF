## Summary

The Strait of Hormuz is the world's most critical energy transit chokepoint, facilitating the daily transit of approximately 20 to 21 million barrels of petroleum liquids—representing roughly 20 to 21 percent of global petroleum liquids consumption—and approximately 20 percent of global liquefied natural gas (LNG) trade, primarily exported by Qatar. At its narrowest point, the strait spans 21 nautical miles, with shipping channels limited to two two-mile-wide navigation lanes separated by a two-mile buffer zone. Because alternative bypass pipeline infrastructure (such as Saudi Arabia's East-West Pipeline and the UAE's Habshan-Fujairah pipeline) can only accommodate approximately 6 to 7 million barrels per day combined, any prolonged blockage or disruption of Hormuz immediately creates severe global energy supply deficits and price spikes.

## Developments

### 2026-08-01

The US Energy Information Administration (EIA) published an updated World Oil Transit Chokepoints assessment, reporting that Hormuz petroleum flows averaged 20.2 million barrels per day, with over 80 percent of exported crude destined for Asian markets including China, India, Japan, and South Korea.

### 2026-06-20

The International Energy Agency (IEA) issued a market commentary warning that ongoing shipping risks and insurance premiums in the Persian Gulf reduce effective global spare capacity buffers and elevate landed crude costs.

### 2026-03-12

Bloomberg and Lloyd's List reported that Joint War Committee (JWC) listed areas in the Persian Gulf led maritime insurers to increase additional war-risk premiums for Very Large Crude Carriers (VLCCs) transiting the Strait of Hormuz.
