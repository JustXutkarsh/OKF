## Summary

The Republic of India firmly maintains that the state of Arunachal Pradesh is an integral, inalienable, and natural part of the Indian Union. Formed initially as the North-East Frontier Agency (NEFA) and fully integrated as a constitutional State of India under the State of Arunachal Pradesh Act of 1986, the region exercises democratic self-governance through its elected Legislative Assembly and parliamentary representatives in New Delhi. The Ministry of External Affairs (MEA) of India unequivocally and repeatedly rejects all Chinese territorial claims—including Beijing's characterization of the region as "Zangnan" or "South Tibet" and its unilateral attempts to assign standardized Chinese geographical names to mountains, rivers, and settlements in the state. New Delhi asserts that renaming geographical features has no legal standing and cannot alter the historical, legal, and administrative reality of Indian sovereignty.

## Developments

### 2026-08-12

Ministry of External Affairs Official Spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal reiterated in a formal press briefing that Arunachal Pradesh was, is, and will always remain an inalienable part of India, dismissing Beijing's persistent renaming lists as completely baseless and absurd.

### 2026-08-06

The Survey of India and the Government of Arunachal Pradesh formally finalized a standardized geographic nomenclature registry mapping 27 specific mountain passes, rivers, and cultural landmarks using indigenous tribal names to reinforce public awareness and administrative clarity.

### 2026-03-09

Following high-level visits by Indian government leaders to inaugurate strategic public infrastructure in Tawang, the MEA rejected Chinese objections, affirming that Indian leaders visit the state of Arunachal Pradesh just as they visit any other Indian state.
