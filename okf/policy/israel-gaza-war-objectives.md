## Summary

The Government of Israel has defined three core war objectives for its campaign in the Gaza Strip: (1) dismantling Hamas's military and governing capabilities to ensure Gaza never again poses a security threat to Israel; (2) securing the return of all living and deceased hostages abducted on October 7, 2023; and (3) establishing overarching, indefinite Israeli security control over the Gaza Strip, including full demilitarization, perimeter buffer zones, and physical control over external borders (the Philadelphi Corridor) to prevent arms smuggling. Israeli leadership firmly rejects any unilateral restoration of the Palestinian Authority in its current form to govern Gaza, and insists that full disarmament must precede any complete military withdrawal or permanent cessation of hostilities.

## Developments

### 2026-08-10

The Prime Minister's Office (PMO) of Israel issued a formal policy statement reiterating that Israel will not agree to a permanent ceasefire or total withdrawal until Hamas has been completely disarmed and verified demilitarization mechanisms are enacted.

### 2026-06-04

Times of Israel and Haaretz covered extensive security cabinet debates regarding "the day after," focusing on proposals for local non-Hamas Palestinian civil administration supported by moderate Arab states alongside an IDF security perimeter.

### 2026-03-22

The Institute for National Security Studies (INSS) in Tel Aviv published a strategic policy roadmap outlining operational benchmarks required for transition from high-intensity maneuver to targeted counter-terror strikes in Gaza.
