## Summary

The caretaker Government of the Republic of Lebanon, headed by Prime Minister Najib Mikati, navigates severe domestic economic collapse, institutional paralysis (prolonged presidential vacancy), and deep political fractures regarding the presence and autonomous military authority of Hezbollah. The official policy of the Lebanese State is centered on advocating for the comprehensive, reciprocal implementation of UNSCR 1701, demanding an immediate halt to Israeli airstrikes and airspace violations, and resolving disputed territorial points along the Blue Line (including the northern sector of Ghajar, the Shebaa Farms, and Kfar Chouba Hills). The Lebanese Armed Forces (LAF), recognized domestically and internationally as the sole legitimate national defense institution, currently maintains units deployed in southern Lebanon alongside UNIFIL. However, the LAF faces critical logistical and financial constraints, requiring extensive international donor assistance to expand its force deployment by an additional 10,000 to 15,000 soldiers to effectively secure the area south of the Litani River.

## Developments

### 2026-07-25

The Lebanese Presidency of the Council of Ministers published a policy declaration affirming Lebanon's readiness to reinforce LAF deployments across southern Lebanon in close coordination with UNIFIL upon the cessation of Israeli military operations.

### 2026-05-08

The Lebanese Armed Forces General Directorate hosted an international military donor coordination conference in Beirut, receiving commitments from the US, France, and Qatar for soldier salary supplements, fuel, and communications gear.

### 2026-02-19

The United States Institute of Peace (USIP) published a study analyzing the LAF's institutional resilience, highlighting its critical role in preventing inter-sectarian conflict despite severe national economic hardship.
