## Summary

Diplomatic efforts to secure a durable ceasefire in the Gaza Strip have been mediated through intensive trilateral diplomacy led by the State of Qatar, the Arab Republic of Egypt, and the United States, backed by United Nations Security Council Resolution 2735. The proposed phased framework structures an initial six-week temporary ceasefire, the surge of hundreds of daily humanitarian aid trucks, the release of vulnerable Israeli hostages (women, elderly, wounded) in exchange for hundreds of Palestinian security prisoners, and the withdrawal of IDF forces from densely populated areas. Transitioning from Phase 1 to Phase 2 (a permanent end to hostilities and complete military withdrawal) remains the primary deadlock: Hamas demands explicit, binding guarantees that Phase 1 leads directly to a permanent end to the war, whereas Israel insists on retaining the right to resume military operations if demilitarization benchmarks are breached.

## Developments

### 2026-08-11

The Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Qatar and Egypt's State Information Service issued a joint statement urging all parties to bridge remaining gaps regarding the phased implementation roadmap and security arrangements along the Gaza-Egypt border.

### 2026-07-29

International mediators submitted an updated bridging proposal detailing specific ratios for hostage-to-prisoner exchanges and establishing an international monitoring mechanism to verify demilitarization and reconstruction aid flows.

### 2026-06-10

The United Nations Security Council adopted Resolution 2735 with 14 votes in favor, endorsing the three-phase comprehensive ceasefire deal and calling on both Israel and Hamas to implement its terms without delay.
