## Summary

United States policy toward the Islamic Republic of Iran combines rigorous economic containment, dynamic military forward deterrence, and restricted diplomatic signaling. Washington utilizes comprehensive financial sanctions under the Countering America's Adversaries Through Sanctions Act (CAATSA) and executive orders to depress Iranian export revenues and constrain defense industrial procurement. Concurrently, US Central Command (CENTCOM) deploys naval carrier strike groups, fifth-generation fighter squadrons (F-22, F-35), and land-based missile defense batteries (Patriot, THAAD) across Gulf partner facilities to deter attacks against US personnel and international shipping. Diplomatic messaging is maintained primarily through Swiss and Omani intermediary channels to establish clear red lines against direct military escalation or attacks on commercial maritime corridors.

## Developments

### 2026-07-20

The US Department of State and the National Security Council reiterated that the United States remains committed to defending its forces and commercial transit rights in international waters, affirming that any attempts to close the Strait of Hormuz will meet swift coalition responses.

### 2026-05-14

The Council on Foreign Relations (CFR) published a policy review assessing the efficacy of US maximum pressure sanctions, noting that while oil revenues were curtailed, illicit maritime trade networks continued delivering crude to private refiners abroad.

### 2026-01-18

US and Iranian diplomatic teams exchanged confidential non-papers via Omani diplomats regarding maritime incident management rules and mutual de-escalation parameters.
