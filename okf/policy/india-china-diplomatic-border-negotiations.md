## Summary

Diplomatic and military dialogue between the Republic of India and the People's Republic of China is conducted primarily through two institutional frameworks: the diplomatic Working Mechanism for Consultation and Coordination on India-China Border Affairs (WMCC), established in 2012, and the high-level Senior Highest Military Commanders' meetings (Corps Commander level talks). Led by joint secretary-level diplomats from India's Ministry of External Affairs and China's Ministry of Foreign Affairs, WMCC sessions focus on maintaining peace and tranquility along the Line of Actual Control, establishing disengagement protocols, instituting temporary non-patrolling buffer zones at friction points, and implementing existing bilateral border agreements (including the 1993 Peace and Tranquility Agreement, the 1996 Military CBMs Agreement, and the 2005 Protocol). While discussions have achieved disengagement in specific Western Sector areas (such as Pangong Tso, Galwan, and Gogra-Hot Springs), progress in the Eastern Sector remains constrained by fundamental disagreements over territorial sovereignty and differing perceptions of the LAC alignment.

## Developments

### 2026-08-08

The 30th meeting of the Working Mechanism for Consultation and Coordination on India-China Border Affairs (WMCC) was held in New Delhi, where both delegations agreed to uphold peace and tranquility in border areas in accordance with bilateral agreements and continue diplomatic and military communication channels.

### 2026-07-04

External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar and Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi held high-level bilateral discussions on the sidelines of the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO) summit in Astana, emphasizing the mutual necessity of early, complete disengagement along all remaining friction points as a prerequisite for the normalization of bilateral ties.

### 2026-03-27

The Ministry of Foreign Affairs of the PRC released a statement reaffirming that boundary questions should be placed in an appropriate position in bilateral relations, calling for mutual accommodation and practical dialogue.
