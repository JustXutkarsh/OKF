## Summary

The member states of the Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC)—notably Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, and the Sultanate of Oman—have pursued a dual-track strategy toward the Islamic Republic of Iran. While maintaining deep security partnerships and defense pacts with the United States, Riyadh and Abu Dhabi have prioritized direct diplomatic de-escalation channels with Tehran following the 2023 Beijing-brokered diplomatic normalization between Saudi Arabia and Iran. GCC states seek to insulate critical domestic infrastructure, maritime ports, and energy export facilities from regional military crossfire. Oman continues to serve as an indispensable confidential intermediary for indirect US-Iran messaging and prisoner exchange negotiations.

## Developments

### 2026-07-15

The International Crisis Group (ICG) published an analysis documenting continued high-level security consultations between Saudi and Iranian foreign ministry delegations aimed at maintaining regional non-aggression commitments and Red Sea maritime stability.

### 2026-05-22

The Emirates News Agency (WAM) reported that UAE diplomatic leadership reiterated commitments to peaceful dispute resolution, commercial maritime stability, and expanded economic backchannels across the Persian Gulf.

### 2026-02-10

Omani diplomatic envoys hosted indirect technical discussions in Muscat addressing maritime incident prevention protocols and maritime communication channels between Persian Gulf littoral states.
