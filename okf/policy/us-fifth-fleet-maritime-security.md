## Summary

United States Naval Forces Central Command (NAVCENT), headquartered in Manama, Bahrain, commands the US Fifth Fleet and oversees multinational maritime coalitions in the Persian Gulf, Gulf of Oman, Red Sea, and Arabian Sea. Central to NAVCENT's posture is the International Maritime Security Construct (IMSC) and its operational arm, Coalition Task Force (CTF) Sentinel, formed to deter state-sponsored harassment of commercial shipping in critical waterways including the Strait of Hormuz and the Bab el-Mandeb. NAVCENT also operates Task Force 59, integrating unmanned surface vehicles (USVs), artificial intelligence sensor networks, and airborne surveillance to build comprehensive maritime domain awareness.

## Developments

### 2026-07-28

NAVCENT issued an operational update confirming expanded 24-hour patrols by IMSC surface combatants and Task Force 59 Saildrone and Devil Ray uncrewed vessels throughout the Strait of Hormuz inbound and outbound transit lanes.

### 2026-05-10

The US Department of Defense and US Central Command (CENTCOM) conducted combined air and maritime defense exercises with regional partner nations to test rapid-response interception of anti-ship cruise missiles and hostile drone swarms.

### 2026-03-14

The Congressional Research Service (CRS) updated its report on US naval presence in the Middle East, detailing Fifth Fleet carrier strike group rotations, destroyer escorts, and interoperability with Gulf Cooperation Council navies.
