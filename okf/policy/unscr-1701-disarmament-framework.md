## Summary

United Nations Security Council Resolution 1701 (UNSCR 1701), unanimously adopted on August 11, 2006 to terminate the Second Lebanon War, serves as the core international legal framework governing security arrangements along the Israel-Lebanon border. Key mandates of Resolution 1701 require: (1) full cessation of hostilities; (2) the establishment between the Blue Line and the Litani River (roughly 30 kilometers north of the border) of an area free of any armed personnel, assets, and weapons other than those of the Government of Lebanon and UNIFIL; (3) the complete disarmament of all non-state armed groups in Lebanon (reaffirming the 1989 Taif Accords and UNSCR 1559); and (4) the prevention of unauthorized arms transfers into Lebanon. In practice, implementation has suffered profound deficits: Hezbollah steadily built extensive military stockpiles and military positions throughout southern Lebanon, while Lebanon and the UN cite continuous Israeli military overflights and violations of Lebanese airspace.

## Developments

### 2026-08-01

The United Nations Security Council conducted its annual review and debate on the mandate renewal of the United Nations Interim Force in Lebanon (UNIFIL), calling for full, unhindered enforcement of Resolution 1701 provisions by all parties.

### 2026-04-16

Security Council Report published its monthly forecast on Lebanon, documenting persistent operational obstacles faced by UNIFIL peacekeepers in accessing private and gated properties across southern Lebanon suspected of housing concealed military launch sites.

### 2026-01-10

Chatham House published a policy study evaluating international diplomatic options for strengthening 1701 verification, proposing expanded joint patrols between UNIFIL and newly equipped Lebanese Armed Forces brigades.
