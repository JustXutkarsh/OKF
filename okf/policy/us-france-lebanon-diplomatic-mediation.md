## Summary

Diplomatic efforts to avert a full-scale regional war between Israel and Lebanon are spearheaded by high-level diplomatic initiatives led by US Senior Presidential Advisor Amos Hochstein and the French Ministry for Europe and Foreign Affairs. The proposed Western diplomatic roadmap is structured in three phased steps: (1) an immediate cessation of cross-border military strikes and the repositioning of Hezbollah heavy weaponry and Radwan fighters approximately 8 to 10 kilometers north of the Blue Line; (2) the deployment of 15,000 Lebanese Armed Forces (LAF) soldiers into southern sectors alongside enhanced UNIFIL freedom of movement and international monitoring; and (3) long-term diplomatic negotiations to resolve all 13 disputed points along the land border (demarcating the permanent international land boundary, modeled on the successful 2022 US-mediated maritime boundary agreement), coupled with an international economic investment package for southern Lebanon's reconstruction.

## Developments

### 2026-08-04

Reuters reported on updated shuttle diplomacy meetings conducted by US and French envoys in Beirut and Tel Aviv, presenting revised monitoring protocols to verify the non-reentry of armed fighters into border zones.

### 2026-05-20

The French Ministry for Europe and Foreign Affairs convened an international ministerial meeting in Paris to coordinate humanitarian relief, economic stabilization, and security equipment support for the Lebanese Armed Forces.

### 2026-02-05

US diplomatic envoys presented a formal non-paper to the Lebanese Speaker of Parliament and the caretaker Prime Minister detailing technical steps for land border demarcation and civilian reconstruction corridors.

## Key Actors

- US Department of State (Special Presidential Envoy Amos Hochstein)
- French Ministry for Europe and Foreign Affairs (Élysée Special Envoys)
- Lebanese Parliament (Speaker Nabih Berri as intermediary)
- Israeli National Security Council (NSC)
