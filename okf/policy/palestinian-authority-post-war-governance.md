## Summary

The future political and administrative governance of the Gaza Strip remains intensely contested among Palestinian factions, regional Arab states, Israel, and the international community. The Palestinian National Authority (PA), headquartered in Ramallah under President Mahmoud Abbas, has formed a technocratic cabinet led by Prime Minister Mohammad Mustafa designed to enact institutional anti-corruption reforms, administrative modernization, and emergency humanitarian relief readiness. The PA maintains that Gaza and the West Bank constitute an indivisible geographic and political unit that must be governed under unified Palestinian institutions. However, significant obstacles persist: Israeli political leadership rejects PA assumption of control without deep structural disarmament, internal Palestinian political reconciliation between Fatah and Hamas remains incomplete, and regional Arab donors insist on clear accountability mechanisms before committing billions in reconstruction funding.

## Developments

### 2026-08-03

WAFA News Agency reported on the Palestinian Cabinet's administrative session in Ramallah, presenting a detailed institutional blueprint for the phased resumption of civil service, municipal utilities, and civil defense management in Gaza under international donor oversight.

### 2026-05-16

The Arab League Summit in Manama adopted a joint resolution calling for the immediate deployment of a United Nations-mandated international peacekeeping and stabilization force in the occupied Palestinian territory until a two-state solution is implemented.

### 2026-02-11

The International Crisis Group (ICG) published a policy brief on Palestinian administrative reform, evaluating the institutional capacity of PA security forces and technocratic agencies to assume civilian policing responsibilities in post-conflict Gaza.
