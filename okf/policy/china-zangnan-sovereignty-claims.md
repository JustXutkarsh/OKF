## Summary

The official position of the People's Republic of China (PRC) asserts that the region administered by India as Arunachal Pradesh is historically and legally Chinese territory, designated by Beijing as "Zangnan" (South Tibet) under the administrative jurisdiction of the Tibet Autonomous Region. China's claim is rooted in its assertion of historic administrative, cultural, and spiritual ties between Tibetan authorities in Lhasa (notably the Tawang Monastery) and the central Qing imperial court. Beijing rejects the legitimacy of the 1914 Simla Convention and the McMahon Line, arguing that the Tibetan local representatives lacked sovereign treaty-making authority to transfer territory to British India. As part of its sovereign claims and cartographic normalization, the Chinese Ministry of Civil Affairs periodically releases official gazetted lists of "standardized geographical names" for mountain peaks, rivers, passes, and settlements across Zangnan in Chinese characters, Tibetan script, and Pinyin.

## Developments

### 2026-08-05

The Ministry of National Defense of the PRC issued an official briefing reiterating that Zangnan has been an inherent part of Chinese territory since ancient times, stating that China firmly opposes India's establishment of the so-called "Arunachal Pradesh."

### 2026-04-01

The Ministry of Civil Affairs of the PRC published its fifth batch of standardized geographical names for Zangnan, adding 30 newly standardized place names, mountain peaks, and water systems to official state cartographic databases.

### 2024-03-28

Spokespersons from the Chinese Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Ministry of National Defense strongly condemned the commissioning of infrastructure projects in the Tawang area, lodging formal diplomatic protests with New Delhi.
