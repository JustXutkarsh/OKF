## Summary

United States policy regarding Taiwan is grounded in the Taiwan Relations Act (TRA) of 1979 (Public Law 96-8), the Three US-China Joint Communiqués, and the Six Assurances provided to Taipei in 1982. Under this framework, Washington maintains robust unofficial relations with Taiwan, opposes any unilateral changes to the status quo by either side, and does not support Taiwan independence while insisting that cross-strait differences be resolved peacefully. Section 2 of the TRA mandates that the United States provide Taiwan with defense articles and services necessary to enable Taiwan to maintain a sufficient self-defense capability, and declares that any effort to determine Taiwan's future by other than peaceful means would constitute a grave concern to the United States. To accelerate defense deliveries, Washington increasingly employs Presidential Drawdown Authority (PDA) and Foreign Military Financing (FMF) alongside conventional Foreign Military Sales (FMS).

## Developments

### 2026-07-18

The US Department of State and the American Institute in Taiwan (AIT) reaffirmed US commitments to cross-strait peace and stability, emphasizing that bilateral trade agreements and defense training missions continue in strict accordance with the US One-China Policy.

### 2026-05-02

The US Defense Security Cooperation Agency (DSCA) notified Congress of approved military equipment packages for Taiwan, including advanced radar communications suites, MQ-9B SeaGuardian uncrewed systems, and NASAMS air defense components.

### 2026-02-19

Congressional Research Service (CRS) updated its legal and strategic overview of the Taiwan Relations Act, detailing bilateral security cooperation, high-level congressional delegations, and maritime deterrence in the Western Pacific.
