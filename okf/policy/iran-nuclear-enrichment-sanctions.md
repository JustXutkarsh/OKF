## Summary

Iran's nuclear program has reached unprecedented technological thresholds following the breakdown of the Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action (JCPOA). According to International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) quarterly verification reports, Iran maintains stockpiles of uranium enriched up to 60 percent purity at the underground Fordow Fuel Enrichment Plant (FFEP) and Natanz Pilot Fuel Enrichment Plant (PFEP). While weaponization requires enrichment to 90 percent U-235 (weapons-grade), nuclear nonproliferation experts estimate Iran's theoretical breakout time—the time required to produce sufficient fissile material for a single nuclear explosive device—at approximately one to two weeks, although constructing a deliverable warhead would require several additional months. In response, the US Department of the Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) continues to enforce extensive secondary sanctions targeting Iran's shadow fleet of oil tankers, illicit financial intermediaries, and procurement networks.

## Developments

### 2026-08-05

The IAEA Board of Governors reviewed the Director General's verification report, confirming continued operation of advanced IR-4 and IR-6 centrifuge cascades at Fordow and expressing concern regarding restricted inspector access under Modified Code 3.1.

### 2026-06-28

The US Department of the Treasury issued additional sanctions designations against dozens of maritime logistics companies and front entities operating across the UAE, Hong Kong, and the Marshall Islands involved in shipping Iranian petroleum to foreign buyers.

### 2026-03-20

The Arms Control Association published a technical assessment showing that while Iran possess sufficient 60-percent enriched uranium to feed rapid 90-percent enrichment runs, international intelligence agencies have not observed active nuclear weaponization directives.
