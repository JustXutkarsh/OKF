## Summary

United States policy on the Gaza conflict balances unwavering support for Israel's legitimate security requirements with extensive diplomatic pressure to surge humanitarian assistance, minimize civilian casualties, and establish a viable political pathway toward durable regional stability. The US Department of State and the National Security Council (NSC) have rejected any long-term Israeli military reoccupation of Gaza, reduction in Gaza's territorial perimeter, or forced displacement of Palestinians. Washington's stated post-conflict vision hinges on reunifying the Gaza Strip and the West Bank under a reformed, "revitalized" Palestinian Authority (PA), integrating international security and stabilization forces, and leveraging regional normalization agreements between Israel and Arab states (particularly Saudi Arabia) contingent upon tangible steps toward a sovereign Palestinian state.

## Developments

### 2026-07-29

The US Department of State and White House National Security Advisor held bilateral consultations with Israeli and Arab security chiefs to advance post-conflict stabilization planning and coordinate humanitarian aid delivery logistics via land and maritime crossings.

### 2026-05-14

US Secretary of State concluded his eleventh regional diplomatic tour across Cairo, Doha, Riyadh, and Tel Aviv, reiterating that long-term Israeli security cannot be achieved without a credible political pathway for Palestinian self-determination.

### 2026-01-20

Carnegie Endowment for International Peace published a comprehensive strategic analysis detailing the operational, political, and financial prerequisites for US-led regional stabilization and reconstruction in post-war Gaza.
