## Summary

The official policy of the People's Republic of China (PRC) maintains that Taiwan is an inalienable part of China's sovereign territory, and that achieving complete national reunification is an essential requirement for the "Great Rejuvenation of the Chinese Nation." Beijing's policy is anchored in the "One-China Principle" and the 1992 Consensus, which asserts that both sides of the Taiwan Strait belong to one China. China's legal framework for cross-strait relations is defined by the 2005 Anti-Secession Law, whose Article 8 states that the state shall employ non-peaceful means and other necessary measures if "Taiwan independence" secessionist forces cause the fact of Taiwan's secession from China, or if major incidents entailing Taiwan's secession occur, or if possibilities for peaceful reunification should be completely exhausted. While Beijing states that "peaceful reunification and One Country, Two Systems" is its primary objective, it repeatedly refuses to renounce the use of military force.

## Developments

### 2026-08-02

The Taiwan Affairs Office (TAO) of the State Council and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs reiterated Beijing's firm opposition to external interference and official contact between foreign governments and Taiwan authorities, characterizing cross-strait relations as purely an internal Chinese affair.

### 2026-05-20

Xinhua News Agency published an official commentary emphasizing that the One-China Principle constitutes the political foundation for cross-strait peace, asserting that attempts to solicit foreign support for independence will inevitably face severe countermeasures.

### 2026-01-01

President Xi Jinping's New Year Address reiterated that China will inevitably be reunified, calling on compatriots across the Taiwan Strait to unite in the common cause of national rejuvenation and oppose external meddling.
