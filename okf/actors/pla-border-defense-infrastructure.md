## Summary

The People's Liberation Army Western Theater Command (PLA WTC) and the Tibet Military District oversee a dense, rapidly modernizing military and civilian infrastructure network along the border facing Arunachal Pradesh. A central component of Beijing's frontier strategy is the construction of over 600 "Xiaokang" (moderately prosperous) border defense model villages constructed along disputed and sensitive sectors of the Tibetan frontier. These settlements serve a designated civil-military dual-use function: they house civilian populations and ethnic Tibetan herders subsidized to maintain continuous physical presence, while featuring fortified underground shelters, hardened communication towers, vehicle staging bays, solar power grids, and all-weather paved road connections directly integrated with PLA logistics lines. Concurrently, the PLA has expanded forward dual-use civilian-military airbases in southern Tibet (such as Shigatse, Nyingchi Mainling, Gonggar, and Bangda), constructing hardened aircraft shelters, unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) hangars, and HQ-9 air defense batteries.

## Developments

### 2026-07-27

The US Department of Defense China Military Power Report published satellite imagery analysis detailing the construction of second-line logistics hubs and elevated heliport networks adjacent to Xiaokang border villages in the Shannan and Nyingchi prefectures facing the Eastern LAC.

### 2026-04-08

The Center for Strategic and International Studies (CSIS ChinaPower) released a study tracking PLA airbase and surface-to-air missile site expansions across the Tibetan Plateau, identifying runway lengthenings capable of supporting heavy J-20 stealth fighters and Y-20 strategic airlifters.

### 2026-01-14

The Observer Research Foundation (ORF India) analyzed the strategic implications of China's Land Border Law and dual-use border settlements, highlighting how civilian populations act as first-line sovereign outposts under PLA administrative oversight.
