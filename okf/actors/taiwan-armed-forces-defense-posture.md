## Summary

The Republic of China Armed Forces (Taiwan Armed Forces) maintain an operational doctrine structured around the Overall Defense Concept (ODC), often described as a "porcupine strategy." Acknowledging the PLA's vast numerical and conventional superiority, Taiwan emphasizes survivable, distributed, and asymmetric capabilities: mobile coastal defense cruise missiles (Hsiung Feng II/III and US-supplied Harpoon systems), mobile surface-to-air missile batteries (Sky Bow III and Patriot PAC-3 MSE), sea-denial naval minelayers, uncrewed aerial and naval reconnaissance systems, and dispersed underground hardened shelters. Taiwan has also reinstated one-year compulsory military service, established the All-Out Defense Mobilization Agency, and reformed annual Han Kuang live-fire exercises to focus rigorously on unscripted counter-blockade, decentralized command, and civil resilience scenarios.

## Developments

### 2026-08-04

Taipei Times covered the conclusion of Taiwan's 2026 Han Kuang live-fire maneuvers, which featured real-time critical infrastructure protection drills, emergency airstrip operations on civilian highways, and mass civilian shelter mobilization.

### 2026-06-11

Taiwan's Ministry of National Defense (MND) announced the accelerated domestic production of mobile anti-ship missile launchers and indigenously developed uncrewed surface combat vehicles to reinforce western littoral defense corridors.

### 2026-03-15

RAND Corporation published a defense analysis evaluating Taiwan's reserve mobilization reforms, highlighting substantial progress in urban combat readiness while identifying continuing training bottlenecks and munitions stockpile requirements.
