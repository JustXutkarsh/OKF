## Summary

Lebanese Hezbollah (Hizbullah) is widely assessed as the world's most heavily armed non-state military organization, possessing an estimated arsenal of 120,000 to 150,000 unguided rockets, precision-guided ballistic missiles (including Fateh-110 and Zelzal variants), anti-tank guided missiles (Kornet, Almas), and long-range one-way attack drones (Ababil, Mirsad). Hezbollah's ground force structure includes specialized elite infantry—the Radwan Force—trained for cross-border infiltration, urban warfare, and anti-armor ambushes. Hezbollah's operational doctrine relies on a vast underground tunnel and fortification network spanning southern Lebanon and the Bekaa Valley ("Nature Reserves"), subterranean command bunkers, and concealed launchers embedded within civilian village infrastructure. Hezbollah ties any cessation of hostilities along the Lebanese front strictly to a complete ceasefire in the Gaza Strip.

## Developments

### 2026-08-06

Al-Manar and regional security media broadcast addresses by Hezbollah leadership reaffirming that the Lebanese resistance will continue its military operations as a supportive front until full ceasefire terms are enacted in Gaza.

### 2026-05-15

The Center for Strategic and International Studies (CSIS) Missile Defense Project published an analysis of Hezbollah's precision-strike capabilities, detailing its use of electro-optically guided Almas anti-tank missiles and kamikaze drones targeting IDF radar sites in northern Israel.

### 2026-01-22

The Alma Research and Education Center released a geographical survey mapping Hezbollah's subterranean infrastructure, multi-barrel rocket launch arrays, and forward staging bunkers positioned south of the Litani River.
