## Summary

Hamas (the Islamic Resistance Movement) operates through a dual structure comprising an external Political Bureau (traditionally based in Doha, Qatar, and regional capitals) and an internal military and governance command in the Gaza Strip. The armed wing, the Izz ad-Din al-Qassam Brigades, historically organized into five regional brigades (Northern Gaza, Gaza City, Central, Khan Younis, and Rafah), has undergone substantial decentralization due to intense attrition of senior and mid-level commanders. Following successive leadership transitions, Hamas has integrated its political negotiation delegations with its internal field command. In negotiations, Hamas's core demands center on a comprehensive and permanent end to the war, complete Israeli troop withdrawal from all areas of Gaza, unrestricted humanitarian aid entry, Palestinian reconstruction rights, and a substantial release of Palestinian prisoners in exchange for remaining Israeli hostages.

## Developments

### 2026-08-09

Al Jazeera and regional Arabic media carried official statements from the Hamas Political Bureau confirming adherence to the late-July mediation framework, while rejecting any permanent Israeli military retention of the Philadelphi or Netzarim corridors.

### 2026-05-12

The European Council on Foreign Relations (ECFR) published a political mapping of Palestinian factions, detailing Hamas's internal consultations between its Gaza-based commanders and external leadership regarding post-war technocratic administration proposals.

### 2026-02-18

The Washington Institute for Near East Policy (WINEP) published an intelligence assessment analyzing Hamas's transition from conventional brigade-level formations to dispersed guerilla tactics operating out of damaged urban sectors.
