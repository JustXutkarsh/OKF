## Summary

The Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps Navy (IRGCN) is the primary branch of the Iranian Armed Forces responsible for naval operations in the Persian Gulf and the Strait of Hormuz. Unlike the conventional Islamic Republic of Iran Navy (IRIN), which operates mainly in the Sea of Oman and the northern Indian Ocean, the IRGCN employs an asymmetric anti-access/area-denial (A2/AD) naval doctrine. This strategy integrates hundreds of fast-attack craft (FAC) armed with anti-ship missiles (ASCM), coastal missile batteries, uncrewed surface vessels (USVs), aerial attack drones, and bottom-mounted naval mines concentrated along the Iranian coastline and strategic islands such as Abu Musa and the Greater and Lesser Tunbs.

## Developments

### 2026-08-10

IRNA and Tasnim News Agency carried statements from IRGCN commanders highlighting newly deployed unmanned patrol craft and radar-evading reconnaissance drones stationed across coastal bases along the Strait of Hormuz.

### 2026-05-18

The US Naval Institute (USNI News) published an assessment of IRGCN swarm tactics, noting increased integration of loitering munitions with high-speed catamarans capable of deploying Noor and Ghader anti-ship cruise missiles.

### 2026-02-28

IRGCN coastal defense units staged live-fire maritime maneuvers in the Strait of Hormuz, demonstrating simulated interception of commercial and military vessels entering the Persian Gulf.
