## Summary

Cross-Strait military tensions across the Taiwan Strait have reached historic highs as the People's Liberation Army (PLA) executes normalized high-intensity combat patrols, Air Defense Identification Zone (ADIZ) incursions, and multi-domain joint encirclement exercises surrounding the main island of Taiwan. Following large-scale named maneuvers—including Joint Sword-2024A, Joint Sword-2024B, Strait Thunder, and Justice Mission drills—the PLA has effectively eliminated the informal median line buffer that previously governed maritime and airspace separation. Taiwan's Ministry of National Defense (MND) conducts daily tracking of PLA fighter jets, airborne early warning aircraft, uncrewed combat aerial vehicles (UCAVs), and PLA Navy (PLAN) warships operating in the northern, southwestern, and eastern approaches to Taiwan.

## Developments

### 2026-08-11

Taiwan's Ministry of National Defense released its daily tracking statement reporting 32 PLA aircraft and 8 PLAN vessels operating around Taiwan within a 24-hour window, with 20 sorties crossing the median line into the southwestern and eastern ADIZ.

### 2026-04-20

PLA Eastern Theater Command conducted joint sea-air combat readiness patrols practicing simulated precision strikes against high-value coastal targets and sea-lane blockade operations around major Taiwanese ports.

### 2026-01-05

Defense analysts from the Institute for the Study of War (ISW) documented the expanding role of the China Coast Guard (CCG) operating in coordination with naval combatants in grey-zone law enforcement drills near Kinmen and Matsu.

## Key Actors

- People's Liberation Army (Eastern Theater Command)
- Taiwan Ministry of National Defense (Republic of China Armed Forces)
- China Coast Guard (CCG)
- US Indo-Pacific Command (INDOPACOM)
