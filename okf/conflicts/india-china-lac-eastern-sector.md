## Summary

The Line of Actual Control (LAC) in the Eastern Sector, spanning approximately 1,346 kilometers along the boundary between the Indian state of Arunachal Pradesh and the Tibet Autonomous Region of the People's Republic of China, remains a heavily militarized front characterized by differing perceptions of the alignment. Following the 2020 Galwan Valley clash in the Western Sector and the December 2022 physical clash at Yangtse in the Tawang sector of Arunachal Pradesh, both the Indian Army and the People's Liberation Army (PLA) have reinforced forward deployments with heavy armor, surface-to-air missile regiments, drone surveillance units, and multi-barrel rocket launchers. While regular border personnel meetings (BPM) and protocols aim to prevent armed escalation, frequent close-quarters patrolling, disputed ridge lines, and rapid weather-resistant road construction sustain elevated operational friction.

## Developments

### 2026-08-10

The Hindu and Indian defense sources reported that the Indian Army Eastern Command completed seasonal rotation and high-altitude acclimation of strike corps units stationed across key vantage points in the Kameng and Subansiri border sectors.

### 2026-04-12

Global Times and Chinese defense media reported on PLA Western Theater Command alpine combat readiness drills in southern Tibet, testing integrated electronic countermeasure networks and rapid troop transport via high-altitude rail.

### 2026-01-18

Indian and Chinese local field commanders held a scheduled flag meeting at Bum La in the Tawang sector, reiterating adherence to existing confidence-building protocols and standard operating procedures to maintain peace and tranquility along the frontier.

## Key Actors

- Indian Army (Eastern Command / 3 Corps & 4 Corps)
- People's Liberation Army (Western Theater Command / Tibet Military District)
- Indo-Tibetan Border Police (ITBP)
- Ministry of Defence of India
