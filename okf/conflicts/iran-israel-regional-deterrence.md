## Summary

The strategic standoff between the Islamic Republic of Iran and the State of Israel has evolved from a covert "shadow war" of targeted cyberattacks, sabotage, and proxy skirmishes into a dynamic of direct state-to-state military exchanges. Iran's deterrence posture relies on large stockpiles of medium-range ballistic missiles (such as the Emad, Ghadr, and Kheibar Shekan), cruise missiles, and Shahed-series one-way attack drones. Israel counters through multi-layered integrated air defense systems—including Arrow-3, David's Sling, and Iron Dome—supplemented by intelligence penetration, long-range stealth strike aircraft (F-35I Adir), and regional sensor sharing coordinated by US Central Command (CENTCOM). The confrontation carries extreme risks of unintended escalation across the Levant, Persian Gulf, and Arabian Sea.

## Developments

### 2026-08-08

The Institute for the Study of War (ISW) and Critical Threats Project reported on elevated air defense readiness across Western Iran and Israel following reciprocal military alerts and counter-strike preparations.

### 2026-04-19

International defense analysts documented Israel's demonstrated capability to conduct precision standoff strikes against strategic radar installations and air defense sites protecting Iranian nuclear and military infrastructure.

### 2026-04-14

US Central Command, the Israel Defense Forces, and regional coalition partners successfully intercepted over 95 percent of hundreds of ballistic missiles, cruise missiles, and drones launched from Iranian territory in a mass saturation strike.
