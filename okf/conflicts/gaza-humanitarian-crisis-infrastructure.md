## Summary

The prolonged conflict in the Gaza Strip has resulted in catastrophic humanitarian conditions, severe civilian displacement, and the near-total collapse of critical civil infrastructure. According to assessments by the United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA) and the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC), over 1.9 million people (more than 85 percent of Gaza's population) have been internally displaced, with hundreds of thousands enduring extreme overcrowding in makeshift tent encampments in Al-Mawasi, Deir al-Balah, and Khan Younis. The IPC classifies broad swathes of the population under Phase 4 (Emergency) and Phase 5 (Catastrophe) acute food insecurity. Municipal water, sanitation, and electrical grids have suffered extensive destruction, while operational hospitals face critical deficits of fuel, medical surgical supplies, and clean potable water.

## Developments

### 2026-08-12

UNRWA released its situation report confirming that logistical constraints, fuel rationing, and damaged transit corridors continue to severely limit the daily distribution of emergency food rations, potable water, and hygiene kits across southern and central Gaza.

### 2026-06-25

The Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) Global Initiative published its Special Brief on Gaza, finding that while targeted aid surges temporarily mitigated famine thresholds in northern governorates, food insecurity risks remained acute across the entire Strip.

### 2026-03-18

United Nations OCHA documented that over 65 percent of all housing structures, 84 percent of health facilities, and the majority of water desalination and sewage treatment facilities sustained structural damage or complete destruction.
