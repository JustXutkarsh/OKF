## Summary

The cross-border confrontation between the Israel Defense Forces (IDF) and Lebanese Hezbollah along the UN-demarcated Blue Line has caused extensive tactical attrition, widespread infrastructure destruction, and massive civilian displacement in both northern Israel and southern Lebanon. Following Hezbollah's opening of a "support front" in October 2023, exchanges of artillery, precision anti-tank guided missiles (ATGMs), uncrewed loitering munitions, and aerial strikes have depopulated over 40 Israeli border communities (with over 60,000 residents evacuated from Upper Galilee and the Golan Heights) and displaced over 100,000 Lebanese civilians across southern governorates and the Bekaa Valley. The conflict has progressively expanded in geographic depth, with IDF precision strikes reaching the suburbs of Beirut and Baalbek, while Hezbollah fires extended-range rockets toward military installations in Haifa, Safed, and the Jezreel Valley.

## Developments

### 2026-08-11

UNIFIL (United Nations Interim Force in Lebanon) and international media reported intense reciprocal artillery and air strikes across Sector East and Sector West of the Blue Line, warning of systemic risks to civilian infrastructure and peacekeeping observation posts.

### 2026-06-19

Haaretz and Times of Israel reported that the IDF Northern Command approved operational plans and logistical preparations for expanded offensive maneuvers into southern Lebanon if diplomatic mediation fails to push Hezbollah forces back from the border.

### 2026-03-28

L'Orient Today documented extensive structural and agricultural damage across dozens of Lebanese border municipalities including Kafr Kila, Khiam, and Ayta ash-Shab resulting from concentrated airstrikes and white phosphorus munitions.
